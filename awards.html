The Ballots

Do the awards go to the best players?

“How well do awards and expert opinions in the WNBA reflect players’ actual performance on the court, and what might explain the gaps between recognition and reality?”

Awards are often considered one of the strongest indicators of greatness in professional sports, it is a way to recognize athletes who have had exceptional seasons. Unlike performance statistics, awards reflect the options of voters, and can be seen as a combination of measurable achievement.

In order to understand how recognition in the WNBA is defined, there is a comparison graph on the results of the 2025 “Most Valuable Player” (MVP) voting with several performance metrics. This includes metrics such as scoring, assists, wins, and team success. This examines if expert opinions align with on court productions or if other factors shape recognition.

The MVP voting reflects the performance of the league’s top players. Within A’ja Wilson, Napheesa Collier, and Alyssa Thomas emerged as the league’s top performers each excelling in different aspects of the game. A’ja Wilson combines elite scoring with defensive production. Napheesa Collier has led one of the league’s most successful teams while averaging about 23 points per game. Alyssa Thomas adds on to this by playmaking and leading the league in assists and recording multiple triple doubles. The result of this indicates that voters consider multiple aspects of performance more than individual scoring when assessing player ability.

There is a difference between recognition and statistical performance. Allisha Gray ranked among the league leaders in win shares but received significantly fewer MVP votes than expected. In addition, Breanna Stewart remains one the the league’s most efficient players but received little recognition after missing games during the season. When it comes to awards it is not only statistics. Berri (1999) mentions how a player's value is measured on how much they contribute to the team's success rather than the awards or scoring individually. Another thing to note is how the media attention plays a key role in determining which athlete receives recognition. Combining award voting and player performance data reveals that greatness is not based on statistics alone. It is constructed through the interaction of measurable performance, social media narratives, and institutional factors. This shapes how those accomplishments are valued.
