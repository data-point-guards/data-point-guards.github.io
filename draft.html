The Draft

What does a top draft pick really buy?

“How does securing top three draft picks correlate with changes in home game attendance and broadcast viewership over the subsequent three seasons?”

Draft order is the league’s clearest public forecast of future value. A top-three selection can also create attention beyond the court by giving fans a new reason to follow a team. This visualization compares average home attendance before each top-three selection with attendance three seasons later for draft years from 2010 through 2021.

A top-three draft pick usually fills seats—but not always
Average home attendance in the season before each top-three pick compared with attendance three seasons later, 2010–2021.

Visualization and data details by Ryu Ohnogi on Tableau Public.

Most of the comparisons move toward higher attendance, while several move in the opposite direction. The pattern suggests that a highly drafted player can help bring more people to the arena, but the pick does not guarantee long-term attendance growth.

What the chart can and cannot show

The graph shows a before-and-after relationship, not proof that a single player caused every change. Team performance, venue capacity, marketing, injuries, roster changes, and local interest can also affect attendance. Research likewise finds that draft position is an imperfect prediction of later professional performance (Harris and Berri 2015), so a high pick should be read as an expectation rather than a guaranteed result.

The chart is most useful for showing how often attendance rose after teams invested in a top-three selection and where that pattern did not hold. The limits of the available records and the choices involved in comparing seasons are discussed further in our Data Critique.
