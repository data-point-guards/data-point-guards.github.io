The Draft

What does a top draft pick really buy?

“How does securing top three draft picks correlate with changes in home game attendance over the subsequent three seasons?”

To investigate our first question, we will look at teams who were able to secure the top-three draft player picks. A team that establishes star-driven support works as an economic model that reliably accelerates franchise attendance, proving the WNBA's capabilities independent of the NBA to corporate sponsors (Zimmer, Snyder, and Zimmer. 2025). This influx of audience engagement should directly translate into higher league revenues, new career opportunities for female athletes via increased salaries and improved training infrastructure, yet a restructuring of the WNBA's relationship with the NBA needs to be made in order for this change (Ettienne, L. 2019).

To visualize this phenomenon, we transformed the Across The Timeline dataset to plot franchise arenas alongside localized changes in demand of fan attendance by percentage; visualized by using Tableau. A connected box plot, or dumbbell chart, was chosen because it made it easy to identify two interacting variables beyond the x and y axes —market scale (baseline Average Home Attendance) and growth velocity (increase or decrease).

In the chart, four players from the 2020 draft class show clear signs of an anomaly, due to the COVID-19 bubble that required The entire 2020 WNBA season to be played in a bio-secure bubble, with no fans in attendance. By excluding this anomaly, the visualization reveals a strong positive correlation where 75% of top-three draft picks drive immediate home attendance growth over subsequent seasons. This upward pattern indicates that acquiring elite collegiate talent acts as a powerful economic catalyst, transforming fan excitement into sustained ticket revenue.

The points that had the highest surge in attendance is vividly illustrated by arrivals like Sabrina Lonescu and AD Durr, which acted as immediate ticket-selling catalysts. Conversely, the rare exceptions—where attendance flatlined or declined—highlight that rookies alone cannot overcome external constraints, such as the 2020 “Bubble” restrictions or localized franchise instability. Ultimately, the visualization proves that top-tier draft prospects serve as powerful economic and promotional engines capable of consistently expanding a franchise’s local fanbase.
