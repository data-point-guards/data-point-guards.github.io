The Crowds

Why do some arenas fill while others stay quiet?

“How does WNBA attendance compare between teams in smaller cities and teams in large metropolitan areas?”

WNBA arena occupancy rate by city
Average seat-fill percentage by team and market size. Red = under 1 million residents; yellow = 1–5 million; blue = over 5 million.

Sources: Across the Timeline attendance and venue records and U.S. Census metropolitan-area data. Visualization by Ryu Ohnogi.

*The second level of our research question examines how WNBA attendance varies across metropolitan areas of different sizes. As metropolitan areas vary considerably in population size, we categorized them into three groups: small (under 1M), medium (1M–5M), and large (over 5M) market tier, each assigned a corresponding color.

A few teams appear at multiple points on the map because on occasion, home teams hosted their games in different areas. We kept the venues separate instead of combining their figures because their seating capacities were not the same. THis means Occupancy rates do not paint a full picture of consumer demand. To compare them, we looked at the share of available seats filled rather than attendance totals alone. Note an important caveat to this method; there now exists a silencing of possible market loss where demand exists beyond the capacity of smaller arenas.

Winning fills seats, but mid-size markets outdraw the biggest ones
Average home attendance versus regular-season winning percentage for 120 team-seasons, 2010–2019.

Sources: Across the Timeline attendance and standings and U.S. Census metropolitan-area data. Visualization by Nguyen Ngo.

By plotting WNBA team-season win percentages against home attendance averages we are able to confirm our original finding that market size is non linear and does not dictate arena ticket performance. mid-size markets sit above large markets across the entire range of win percentages, and small markets remain the lowest. This is imperative to highlight because it forestalls the conclusion that there is absolutely no correlation between market size and attendance, but one of the many factors.

All three trend lines slope upward, indicating that winning is associated with higher attendance in every market tier. This indicates Winning has more leverage in selling tickets over market size.

Going back to our map of the dataset, High and low occupancy rates appear across all three population categories, suggesting that metropolitan size alone does not explain how successfully a WNBA team fills its arena and this is confirmed again when plotted on our graph with different information. Previous sports economic literature indicates that WNBA demand depends heavily on localized, game-day dynamics to explain this pattern. Traditional factors affecting demand for professional sporting events include the quality of the home and opposing teams, game promotions, economic considerations, and scheduling convenience. Two factors are especially relevant to this discourse: interactive activities, such as player appearances and fan participation, and advocacy programming connected to community and social causes. (Johnson and Bo, 2023) These components suggest that attendance numbers are driven by the team, game-day experience, and relationship with its community far more than the absolute size of the surrounding metropolitan population.
