The Crowds

Why do some arenas fill while others stay quiet?

“How does WNBA attendance compare between teams in smaller cities and teams in large metropolitan areas?”

A large metropolitan population creates a large potential audience, but it does not guarantee that a team will fill its building. To compare markets fairly, the map uses occupancy rate — the share of available seats filled — rather than raw attendance alone. City population categories come from U.S. Census metropolitan-area data, while attendance and venue records come from Across the Timeline.

WNBA arena occupancy rate by city
Average seat-fill percentage by team and market size. Red = under 1 million residents; yellow = 1–5 million; blue = over 5 million.

Sources: Across the Timeline attendance and venue records and U.S. Census metropolitan-area data. Visualization by Nguyen Ngo.

The map does not produce a simple big-city advantage. Strong occupancy appears in several market tiers, and some large metropolitan areas fill a smaller share of their available seats than teams in mid-sized cities. This agrees with scholarship finding that WNBA demand cannot be explained by population alone (Agha and Berri 2023).

Winning, market size, and the crowd

The next view separates 120 team-seasons from 2010 through 2019 by market size and compares regular-season winning percentage with average home attendance. The upward lines show that winning generally helps, but the vertical separation between the market groups is just as important.

Winning fills seats, but mid-size markets outdraw the biggest ones
Average home attendance versus regular-season winning percentage for 120 team-seasons, 2010–2019.

Sources: Across the Timeline attendance and standings and U.S. Census metropolitan-area data. Visualization by Ryu Ohnogi.

Across this decade, better teams generally drew larger crowds. Yet the trend lines also show that mid-size markets often outdrew the largest markets at similar winning percentages. Market size and team quality matter, but neither operates alone. The live-game experience, promotion, scheduling, and community programming can also shape demand in ways that a population count cannot capture (Agha and Berri 2023; Johnson and Bo 2023).

Average crowds can hide a team’s ceiling

A season average compresses every game into one number. Comparing each team’s average with its highest recorded attendance reveals how much larger the audience can become for a particular opponent, venue, promotion, or moment.

Average versus highest attendance by WNBA team
Each point compares a team’s average crowd with its largest recorded crowd in the assembled attendance records.

Source: Across the Timeline attendance records. Visualization by Mariana Rosas.

The distance between the average and the peak is evidence of unrealized or event-specific demand, not a prediction that every game could reach the same crowd. Arena capacity, neutral-site or relocated games, schedule differences, and reported rather than turnstile attendance limit direct comparison. Taken together, the three views support a careful conclusion: WNBA attendance is connected to winning and market conditions, but city size by itself is not a reliable explanation.
