The Pipeline

Does March Madness stardom survive the jump?

“How does NCAA tournament success translate into a WNBA career?”

Earlier draft picks tend to score more as WNBA rookies
2025 rookie points per game compared with WNBA draft position; color identifies each player’s March Madness result.

Visualization and data details by Ryu Ohnogi on Tableau Public.

One of our research questions examines whether NCAA tournament success translates into professional careers in the WNBA. It is important to consider where many professional careers begin. The NCAA tournament serves as a major pathway into the league by giving college athletes the opportunity to have national exposure to compete with other top college athletes in competitions (Harris & Berri, 2015). This visualization provides whether the NCAA tournament success translates into early opportunities in the WNBA by comparing players' best tournament appearance with the average minutes per game during their rookie season. Players are categorized according to the furthest round reached by their college team advanced in the NCAA tournament, with national champions on the left. The vertical axis represents average WNBA playing time in minutes per game. The color of each circle indicates the draft position. While red indicated earlier draft picks and green indicated later selections.

The visualization suggests that NCAA tournaments success increases a player’s visibility and draft value but it does not consistently predict professional success. One example is analyzing National Champion Paige Bueckers and Sweet 16 participant Sonia Citron were both selected early in the draft with others including Kiki Iriafen and Saniya Rivers, who also received significant playing time. These findings support Harris and Berri’s conclusion that Final Four appearance improved a player’s expected draft positions by nearly six selections, demonstrating that successful NCAA programs increase professional opportunities (Harris & Berri, 2015).

Tournament success alone does not determine a player's WNBA career, however. Players within the same tournament category often experience considerable differences in professional settings and several athletes from teams eliminated in earlier rounds ultimately average more WNBA minutes than players that are in the national championship programs. This analysis goes back to Harris and Berri’s finding that draft position alone is not a strong predictor of future performance (Harris and Berri, 2015). This is similar to Jepsen's finding that, “More productive players, and players drafted in the first round, are less likely to leave the WNBA.” (Jepson, 2023) This adds to the argument that while tournament success and draft status can create initial opportunities, career longevity is dependent upon professional performance rather than college achievements.

From a Digital Humanities perspective, combining the NCAA and WNBA dataset demonstrates how attending a prestigious university, showcasing college achievement, and creating a brand early in your career leads to media exposure and can shape professional opportunities while also revealing that tournament success is an early measure of greatness. The visualization highlights how performance, opportunities, and media visibility can shape WNBA careers.
