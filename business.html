The Business

Teams, markets, and the growth of the league

“How do internal competitive milestones (like top draft picks) and external structural factors (such as market size/ attendance) drive the economic growth and societal impact of women's sports?”

For decades, women’s professional basketball was culturally subsidized by the National Basketball Association (NBA) and operated at a net loss despite public interest which ignited its formation in 1996 (Barnes, K. 2026). Today, the explosive growth of the WNBA has fundamentally shattered this historical paradigm, overhauling the perception of female athletes and creating the opportunity for re-establishment of the WNBA and the players of the league. This sudden transformation made our team wish to explore the interaction of internal competitive milestones, like top draft picks, and external structural factors, such as market size, that drive economic growth and measure its societal impact.
